# Video Story

Turn intent into a brief strong enough to accept or reject moments in the edit. Do not solve an unclear story with pacing tricks or graphics.

## Workflow

1. Inspect provided scripts, briefs, transcripts, references, brand rules, and source constraints.
2. Define audience, existing knowledge, need, intended takeaway, and viewing context.
3. Write the story promise in one sentence and the change from opening state to closing state.
4. Choose the smallest structure that fits the material. Read [story-structures.md](references/story-structures.md) when structure is unresolved.
5. Set a target runtime range based on essential beats, not a universal ideal length.
6. Lock destination, aspect/orientation, tone, evidence rules, exclusions, accessibility needs, and definition of done.
7. Record the result in `../../assets/VIDEO_BRIEF.template.md` and mark assumptions separately from confirmed decisions.

## Lock standard

The brief is ready for editorial when it can answer: who is this for, what must land, what can be cut, how long may it breathe, where will it be watched, how should it feel, and what source or claim must not be distorted?

If inputs are incomplete, produce a proposed brief and identify only the smallest decisions that materially change the cut. Do not ask again for information already explicit in the request or source.

For interviews, plan questions and coverage around story beats without scripting testimony. For documentary, archival, healthcare, research, or case-study work, preserve uncertainty and primary-source authority. Generated examples must remain visibly separate from evidence.

Read [brief-and-preproduction.md](references/brief-and-preproduction.md) for interview, demo, launch, social, and long-form planning checks.
