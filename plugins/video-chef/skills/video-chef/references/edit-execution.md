# Edit execution

## Prepare the plan

Map each locked story beat to dialogue, picture, sound, graphics, source time ranges, and intended duration. Identify the hook, development, transitions, payoff, final takeaway, and deliberate exclusions.

For edits that must remain editable, prefer an NLE-native project or sequence, or a documented interchange. Keep a human-readable cut map beside the project so decisions survive tool or format changes.

When the task requires scripting Premiere Pro, After Effects, Media Encoder, Animate, or Illustrator, use the sibling `adobe-video-automation` skill. It routes to the matching docsforadobe guide and adds version, mutation, and runtime-verification gates.

## Build conservatively

- Start with the strongest continuous story and intelligible dialogue.
- Use B-roll, demos, cutaways, titles, and motion graphics to clarify specific beats or solve visible edits.
- Preserve natural pauses that carry meaning; remove dead time according to the locked tone and runtime.
- Avoid excessive punch-ins, generic kinetic text, transition packs, or constant motion unless the brief calls for them.
- Keep captions within verified safe areas and readable at the actual viewing size. Check line breaks, names, punctuation, timing, and contrast.
- Preserve source cadence unless a deliberate conform is required. Document frame-rate conversion, retiming, stabilization, denoise, restoration, reframing, or identity-affecting treatment.
- Mix dialogue first. Use music and SFX only when authorized and rights are known. Meters do not replace listening.

## Proof before scale

Use a short representative proof when uncertainty involves visual identity, restoration, reframing, color, graphics language, animation, voice processing, music direction, or expensive generation. Compare source and candidate at matched timing, normal speed, intended scale, and sound-on where relevant.

Approval of a proof authorizes only the demonstrated direction unless the user explicitly expands it to the full edit.

## Existing projects

Before modifying an NLE project, identify the canonical project, active sequence or composition, linked media, project version, and output destination. Create a clearly named isolated copy or new sequence for experimental work. Do not flatten the only editable master.

## Render handling

Write renders atomically when possible and wait for the encoder to close the file. Probe the fresh output, decode it end to end, and inspect the rendered result—not a stale preview or source timeline. Keep review proxies distinct from delivery masters.
