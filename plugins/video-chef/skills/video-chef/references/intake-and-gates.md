# Intake and creative lock

Use this for a new edit, substantial recut, or any request where composition and pacing depend on editorial intent.

## Five decisions to lock

### Audience

Define who will watch, what they care about, what they already know, how much context they need, and the one takeaway that should remain.

### Story

Define the opening hook, core message, order of essential moments, deliberate exclusions, and closing takeaway. A collection of good clips is not yet a story.

### Length

Use a target range rather than assuming an ideal duration. Let the story determine the final runtime inside that range. The range controls number of beats, time per beat, removals, and pacing.

### Format

Confirm destination, aspect ratio, orientation, resolution, frame rate/cadence requirements, captions, audio expectations, safe areas, and any platform-specific constraints. Re-check current platform rules when delivery depends on them.

### Feeling

Name the intended experience in concrete editorial terms: rhythm, energy, color, music/sound role, visual density, and moments that should breathe. A short phrase such as “upbeat launch animation” or “documentary-style explanation” is useful when paired with one or two observable traits.

## Lock rule

For a new narrative edit, do not construct the full timeline until these five decisions are either explicitly confirmed by the user or written as a proposed brief with assumptions clearly marked and then confirmed.

Do not block a narrow technical transformation on this lock. Do not ask about decisions already evident from source material or explicit instructions.

## Completion bar

The brief is locked when it is specific enough to decide whether a proposed moment belongs, how long it may stay, where text may sit, and whether the edit feels on-direction. Record later changes as brief revisions so the cut remains explainable.
