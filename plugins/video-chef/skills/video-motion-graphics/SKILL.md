# Video Motion Graphics

Design graphics as part of the edit, not as detached slides. Preserve the subject, source footage, and approved brand decisions.

## Entry gate

Resolve the locked brief, actual footage or edit context, destination aspect and scale, brand assets, required copy, rights, and the beat each graphic supports. If these are missing, create a small proposed direction rather than a full package.

## Workflow

1. Inventory existing typography, palette, logos, templates, motion references, captions, and NLE or After Effects assets.
2. Define a compact system: hierarchy, grid, safe areas, type behavior, color roles, transition grammar, and reduced-motion behavior where applicable.
3. Storyboard only purposeful interventions: identify what the viewer should notice, understand, or remember.
4. Build one representative motion proof over the actual footage at intended aspect, normal speed, and sound-on.
5. Inspect full size and destination scale, including phone scale where relevant. Check collision with captions and platform UI.
6. Obtain direction approval before scaling across the film. Keep proofs, editable components, integrated candidates, and approved masters distinct.

## Rules

- Keep real people and meaningful action visible when they are the narrative anchor.
- Avoid presentation-slide composition, generic kinetic typography, default-looking lower thirds, constant transitions, and decorative motion without an editorial function.
- Use concise on-screen copy. Do not repeat full spoken sentences unless accessibility or comprehension requires it.
- Preserve exact names, titles, product terms, and locked wording.
- Generated imagery must be approved, rights-safe, and clearly separate from archival or captured evidence.
- Technical playback proves only that the animation renders. Judge rhythm, hierarchy, legibility, integration, and emotional fit in the moving edit.
